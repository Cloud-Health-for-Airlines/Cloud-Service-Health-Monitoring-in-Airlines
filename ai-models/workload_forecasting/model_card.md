# BACCP Workload Forecasting — Model Card & Design Document

## 1. Overview & Project Role

- **Module Name:** `baccp-workload-forecasting`
- **Version:** `1.1.0` (Task 1B Verified)
- **Owner / Workstream:** Testbed, eBPF & Backend Workstream (Raghav)
- **Primary Objective:** Deliver reproducible, multi-horizon, per-service incoming workload forecasting ($H=1: 20\text{s}$, $H=5: 100\text{s}$, $H=15: 300\text{s}$) across hybrid airline microservices and gateways, with nominal 90% conformal prediction intervals and signed capacity headroom metrics.
- **Independence:** Strictly decoupled from downstream cascade failure predictor (`cascade_predictor_tier1a.pt`) and PPO circuit breaker (`circuit_breaker_ppo.pt`). Provides upstream incoming demand visibility before downstream degradation occurs.

---

## 2. Data Contract & Provenance

- **Contract Version:** `baccp-workload-contract-v1.1.0`
- **Data Provenance:** Synthetic simulation dataset generated by deterministic CPU-friendly generator (`generate_workload_data.py`).
- **Sampling Interval ($\Delta t$):** 20.0 seconds.
- **Trajectory Duration:** 90 steps ($1,800\text{s} = 30.0\text{ minutes}$).
- **Seasonal Micro-Cycle Period ($S$):** 15 steps ($300\text{s} = 5.0\text{ minutes}$, simulator assumption representing ticketing and scheduled pollers).
- **Physical Quantities & Units:**
  - `offered_workload_rps`: Customer and caller incoming demand (requests/second).
  - `completed_throughput_rps`: Successfully executed transactions (requests/second).
  - `configured_capacity_rps`: Provisioned simulation assumption (requests/second, or None if unknown).
  - `capacity_margin_rps`: $\text{capacity} - \text{forecast}$ (signed: positive under-capacity, negative overload).
  - `available_headroom_rps`: $\max(0, \text{capacity\_margin})$ (requests/second).
  - `overload_rps`: $\max(0, -\text{capacity\_margin})$ (requests/second).
  - `capacity_type`: `"configured_simulation_assumption"` or `"unknown_uninstrumented"`.
- **Configured Capacities (Simulation Assumptions):**
  - `reservations` (cloud-native): 100.0 RPS.
  - `crew` (cloud-native): 60.0 RPS.
  - `baggage` (cloud-native): 75.0 RPS.
  - `boundary-gateway` (boundary gateway): 220.0 RPS.
  - `legacy-core` (legacy mainframe): `None` (explicitly uninstrumented / unknown capacity).

## 3. Ordered Feature Schema & Continuous Inference Phase Handling

- **Feature Schema Version:** `baccp-workload-features-v1.1.0`
- **Feature Count:** Exactly 20 float64 features, strictly ordered:
  1. `lag_0`: Most recent observed offered workload at origin $t$ ($y_t$).
  2. `lag_1` to `lag_5`: Immediate prior lags $y_{t-1} \dots y_{t-5}$.
  3. `lag_9`: Intermediate lag $y_{t-9}$.
  4. `lag_14`: Lag from exactly 1 full micro-cycle ago ($y_{t-14}$).
  5. `mean_3`, `mean_5`, `mean_15`: Rolling window means.
  6. `std_5`, `std_15`: Rolling window standard deviations.
  7. `min_15`, `max_15`: Rolling window extrema.
  8. `diff_1`: Momentum ($y_t - y_{t-1}$).
  9. `diff_5`: Intermediate momentum ($y_t - y_{t-5}$).
  10. `ratio_to_mean15`: Normalised demand ratio ($y_t / (\text{mean}_{15} + 10^{-5})$).
  11. `sin_phase`, `cos_phase`: Periodic micro-cycle phase angles.
- **Continuous Phase Alignment & Variable Buffer Lengths:**
  - Micro-cycle phase $\phi(t) = (t_{\text{elapsed}} / 20\text{s}) \bmod 15$ is anchored to continuous physical time via `timestamp_sec` or discrete `step_index`.
  - Sliding history buffers of variable lengths (e.g. 20, 30, 45 observations) ending at the same physical timestamp produce **bit-for-bit identical feature vectors and identical predictions**.
  - When timestamp context is omitted, the engine uses a neutral phase fallback ($\phi=0$, $\sin=0.0, \cos=1.0$) rather than silently inferring global phase from buffer length, and emits `is_phase_anchored=False` in reliability metadata.

## 4. Incident Splits & Anti-Leakage Controls

Partitioning by incident ID prevents cross-window leakage:
- **Training Set:** 120 incidents (`inc_0000` to `inc_0119`), 7,320 windows per service.
- **Validation Set:** 30 incidents (`inc_0120` to `inc_0149`), 1,830 windows per service.
- **Calibration Set:** 25 incidents (`inc_0150` to `inc_0174`), 1,525 windows per service.
- **Standard Held-Out Test Set:** 25 incidents (`inc_0175` to `inc_0199`), 1,525 windows per service.
- **Diagnostic Unseen Surge Test Set:** 25 incidents (`inc_unseen_0000` to `inc_unseen_0024`), examined in Task 1.
- **Locked Fresh Unseen Surge Test Set:** 25 incidents (`inc_surge_locked_0000` to `inc_surge_locked_0024`), independently seeded (9000–9024) for final locked evaluation.

## 5. Model Selection, Fallback Policy & Uncertainty Calibration

1. **Candidate Models Evaluated:**
   - **Persistence:** $\hat{y}_{t+H} = y_t$.
   - **Seasonal Naive:** $\hat{y}_{t+H} = y_{t+H - S}$ with persistence fallback.
   - **Autoregressive Ridge:** Regularized linear regression on scaled features.
   - **Gradient Boosting:** `HistGradientBoostingRegressor` on lagged and phase features.
2. **Surge Fallback Policy (`auto_fallback`):**
   - Tree-based regressors cannot extrapolate beyond training leaf values. When current workload $y_t > \tau_{\text{train}}$ (maximum workload observed during training for that service), the policy automatically routes to `autoregressive_ridge` for linear extrapolation.
   - Preserves all 4 candidate model forecasts under `candidate_forecasts` for transparent inspection.
3. **Conformal Uncertainty & Reliability Semantics:**
   - Calibrator computes exact finite-sample empirical quantile $k = \lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil - 1$ on absolute residuals in the calibration split, strictly checking $n \ge \lceil 1/\alpha \rceil - 1$.
   - **No False Coverage Guarantees:** Conformal exchangeability requires i.i.d. draws. Autocorrelated time series and flash surges violate exchangeability. Being inside the observed workload range does NOT guarantee interval coverage.
   - The engine flags `exchangeability_assumed = False` and sets `is_composite_policy_calibrated = False` (the switching policy uses component calibrators, not an end-to-end joint calibration).

## 6. Accurate Assessment of Fallback Performance & Limitations

On the fresh locked unseen surge set ($2.2\times - 2.85\times$ surge intensity):
1. **At H=1 (20s):**
   - `auto_fallback` ($\text{MAE} = 18.37\text{ RPS}$) improves massively over unmodified Gradient Boosting ($\text{MAE} = 62.43\text{ RPS}$, $+70.6\%$ error reduction).
   - **However, `auto_fallback` loses to pure Ridge AR ($\text{MAE} = 11.69\text{ RPS}$) and Persistence ($\text{MAE} = 12.71\text{ RPS}$)** because before the $y_t > \tau_{\text{surge}}$ threshold is reached, GB is still active during the surge ramp.
2. **At H=5 (100s):**
   - `auto_fallback` ($\text{MAE} = 66.90\text{ RPS}$) improves over Gradient Boosting ($83.78\text{ RPS}$).
   - **However, `auto_fallback` loses to Persistence ($\text{MAE} = 56.42\text{ RPS}$) and Ridge AR ($\text{MAE} = 61.30\text{ RPS}$)**.
3. **At H=15 (300s):**
   - `auto_fallback` ($\text{MAE} = 90.29\text{ RPS}$) **loses to BOTH pure Ridge AR ($\text{MAE} = 78.78\text{ RPS}$) and unmodified Gradient Boosting ($\text{MAE} = 84.85\text{ RPS}$)**.
4. **Conclusion for Task 2:**
   - The policy is not an all-regime winner. Downstream Task 2 components (degradation prediction and circuit breaker) must consume full `candidate_forecasts` and `reliability_metadata`, treating point forecasts and intervals as uncertain experimental signals.
